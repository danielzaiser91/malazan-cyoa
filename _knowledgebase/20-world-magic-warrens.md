# World, Magic and Metaphysics

`#spoiler:gotm` unless a section says otherwise.

## Warrens — the magic system

A **warren** is both a source of sorcery *and* a real place. A mage with affinity for a warren
opens it by will, draws its power into the world to fuel a spell — or steps bodily through it and
walks, using it as a shortcut across hundreds of leagues of the physical world.

**Design-relevant rules:**

| Rule | Consequence for gameplay |
|---|---|
| Opening a warren is **loud** to anyone sensitive | Stealth and sorcery are opposed choices; a mage character cannot "quietly" solve a scene |
| Most mages hold **one** warren; a rare few hold many (Quick Ben is the famous outlier) | A "warren slot" progression system is canon-legal |
| Warrens can be **poisoned, torn, or in turmoil** | Travel through them is a risk roll, not a free teleport |
| Warrens were **bled into existence by the Elder God K'rul**, from his own body | Wounding the world wounds him — a recurring moral image |
| **Otataral** (a red-rust ore, Seven Cities) deadens magic | The canonical anti-magic item; perfect as a rare, double-edged inventory item |

**The Paths (human-accessible warrens):**

| Warren | Aspect |
|---|---|
| Denul | Healing |
| D'riss | Earth, stone |
| Meanas | Shadow and illusion |
| Mockra | Mind, telepathy, manipulation — subtle, hard to detect |
| Rashan | Darkness proper (not illusion) |
| Ruse | Sea, water |
| Serc | Sky, wind, weather |
| Telas | Fire — overt, impossible to hide |
| Thyr | Light |
| Hood's Path | Death, necromancy |
| The Imperial Warren | A dead, grey warren the Malazan military uses for transport |

**Elder Warrens** are older and tied to a race rather than an aspect: **Kurald Galain** (Tiste Andii,
Darkness), **Kurald Emurlahn** (Tiste Edur, Shadow — shattered into fragments), **Tellann** (Imass,
fire of the earth), **Omtose Phellack** (Jaghut, ice), **Starvald Demelain** (dragons/Eleint).
Before warrens there were the **Holds**, an older and cruder system.

**Erikson's stance:** the magic is deliberately under-explained and shamanic in inspiration.
> "We learned early on to keep it mysterious and mysterious it shall remain." — Steven Erikson

⚠️ **Adaptation warning:** do not build a tidy D&D spell list. The game exposes warrens as
*narrative affordances and risks*, never as a mana bar with tooltips.

## Ascendancy

Mortals, gods, and things in between sit on one ladder. Enough power, enough will, enough
attention from the world — and a being **Ascends**. Ascendants are not automatically gods (a god
needs worshippers); gods are not automatically the strongest Ascendants. Ascendants meddle,
gamble, and get used by mortals as often as the reverse.

This is the canonical justification for a **levelling system**: the player's rise is literally
called Ascendancy in-world, and it is dangerous — power draws divine attention, and being noticed
by gods is, as a Bridgeburner tells young Paran in the prologue, the thing you least want.

## The Deck of Dragons

A tarot-like deck of ~7 Houses plus a large body of **Unaligned** cards. Each card is a *role*
inside a House (e.g. King, Knight, Mason, Virgin, Consort of High House Death), and the cards are
alive in the sense that they change as the pantheon changes; new cards appear when new powers rise.

Named Houses across the series: **Life, Death, Light, Dark, Shadow, and Chains** (the last is the
Crippled God's House and appears later than Book 1). Famous Unaligned: **Oponn** (the twin Jesters
of Chance, Pull and Push), **the Rope**, **Obelisk**, **Chain**, **Crown**, **Sceptre**, **Orb**.

A reading is not a prophecy engine. It shows *fields of force* — who is moving, who is converging,
who is hiding. Tattersail lays out a reading in Book 1 and it points to Anomander Rake and Oponn.

⚠️ **Best structural idea in the whole knowledgebase:** the Deck *is* the story graph. See the
design plan — the in-fiction Deck reading and the out-of-fiction flowchart are the same UI.

## Azath Houses

Living structures that appear where power concentrates. They grow, they seize dangerous
Ascendants, and they hold them in their grounds as barrow-yards, forever. They do not negotiate.
An Azath is the world's own immune system — and the perfect fail-state that is worse than death.

## Convergence

A named phenomenon: power draws power. When enough Ascendants, armies, and artefacts move toward
the same event, the convergence becomes inevitable and everyone gets pulled in, whatever they
intended. Structurally this is Erikson's plot engine — many threads, one collision.

**This is the game's word for a bottleneck node.** Adopt it literally.
