# The Malazan Book of the Fallen — Series Overview

`#spoiler:series` — do not use anything from here in Book 1 content without checking scope.

## The shape of the thing

Ten novels by **Steven Erikson** (pen name of Canadian archaeologist/anthropologist Steve Lundin),
1999–2011, roughly 3.3 million words. Erikson has called the series a plea for compassion running
to three and a half million words — that sentence is the design brief for the whole game.

Unusual for epic fantasy: the books are **mostly self-contained novels** sharing a world, a
timeline and a subset of characters, rather than one story sliced into ten. Books 2 and 3 run
**concurrently on different continents**. The nearest thing to a spine is the imprisoned alien
deity, the **Crippled God**, and the slow question of what to do about him.

The world was co-created with **Ian C. Esslemont**, who writes his own novels in it (*Night of
Knives*, *Return of the Crimson Guard*, …). Those are **out of scope** for this game unless the
player-facing codex needs a footnote.

## The through-line, stated plainly

An alien god was torn from his own realm and chained into this world by a ritual of mortal
sorcerers. He is in constant agony. Agony makes him poison the warrens and cultivate followers out
of the desperate and the maimed. Over ten books the question shifts from *how do we kill him* to
*what would it cost to show him mercy* — and the answer is an army that marches without being told
why, for people who will never know their names.

## Why it is hard to adapt (read this before designing)

- **No protagonist.** Dozens of POVs, many of whom never meet. A single-PC adaptation has to solve
  this deliberately — see the design plan's dual-track PC model.
- **Deliberate disorientation.** Erikson drops you mid-history with no glossary and expects you to
  catch up. A game cannot do that; it needs a **Codex** that fills in silently as terms appear.
- **Convergence structure.** Threads collide at set pieces. That maps *beautifully* onto
  branch-and-bottleneck: the collisions are the bottlenecks.
- **Everyone is grey.** Marines joke through atrocity; the empire is both the villain and the home
  team. Choices must never be signposted good/evil.
- **The tone swings hard** — Kruppe's comic self-narration sits three pages from a massacre. Keep
  the swing; flattening it is the most common way adaptations fail.
